# Option C — Insensitivity of ES-TRMP's destroy-and-repair mutation to destruction size

Status: write-up of a **negative / null result from a κ-only diagnostic**. Every number below is reproduced from the logs listed in §7.
It is a draft section for a paper, not a finished paper, and it covers **one aperture and one instance per size** (see §5).

## Abstract

ES-TRMP (Srivastava & Singh 2018) fixes the destruction size of its destroy-and-repair mutation at κ=4 and draws the destroyed groups uniformly at random.
We asked whether a controller that adapts κ could have anything to exploit. We instrumented the published algorithm (reproduced exactly on 32/32 instances
checked) and ran it with fixed κ ∈ {2,4,6,8} on three instances (n=100, 198, 400), paired by seed, comparing at **equal numbers of candidate evaluations**.
Findings: (i) 80–99 % of mid-run offspring have exactly the parent's cost; larger κ adds mostly *worse* offspring and almost no extra improvements, while
multiplying the cost per mutation by about κ/2. (ii) At equal evaluation budget the best-so-far differs between κ values by less than the run-to-run noise at n=100 and n=198
(all between-κ comparisons at the full budget have p ≥ 0.27 with 10 paired runs; every κ ends within 0.1 % of κ=4 at n=198), and only a weak, sign-unstable difference
appears at n=400 (3 runs). (iii) Among the tie offspring, most are **not** the identical permutation: at κ=4 only 3.9 % (n=100) and 16.4 % (n=198) of mutations return the parent
permutation unchanged; the remaining ties are different permutations of equal cost ("plateau moves"), which the code accepts. Within the tested family, the landscape is therefore
insensitive to destruction size at this budget; an adaptive-κ controller has at most ≈0.2 % headroom at n=198 on this evidence.

## 1. Problem

The repair operator of ES-TRMP is greedy and problem-specific; the destroy step is blind (uniform draw, κ constant for the whole run, plus a stagnation-triggered
large-κ "shuffle" governed by η=100 and the δ ladder). The design document (Differential_RL_TRMP, §3.4) identifies that blind draw as the single leverage point for a
learned controller, and its §25 says to measure whether the landscape carries any signal **before** building one. This section is that measurement for the
simplest action set (κ only).

Question: *does any fixed κ ∈ {2,4,6,8} reach a better best-so-far than κ=4 at the same number of objective evaluations?* If not, a learned κ schedule can only
gain by switching between κ values at different phases, and the diagnostic gives a direct upper-bound-style picture of how much that could be worth.

## 2. Method

**Instrumented baseline.** `src/diag_es.c` is generated from the untouched `es_pre_sfFIN.c` by `scripts/make_diag.py` (only adds an evaluation counter, a κ command-line option
and a per-mutation log). `src/diag_noop.c` (generated by `scripts/make_diag_noop.py`) additionally logs whether the offspring permutation equals the parent permutation.
Pre-processing (NEH + opposite solutions, χ=20), repair, acceptance rule (ties accepted), 4,000 iterations and the η=100/δ-ladder shuffle are unchanged.
Only the value of κ used by the ordinary mutation changes (the shuffle uses its own δ). The no-op patch was checked not to alter the search: the final best of runs 0–2
is identical to the earlier logs in all 6 (instance, κ) cells.

**Evaluation unit.** One *candidate evaluation* = one call of `findcost_d` (a candidate insertion position). Mutation cost is Σ(n−κ+i), i=1..κ, calls (e.g. 786 at n=198, κ=4 measured).
The original paper has no such counter (it stops at 4,000 iterations and reports CPU seconds), so equal-iteration comparison would hand κ=8 about 4× the budget of κ=2.

**Design.** Instances (all aperture φ=π/3, i.e. the `s06` family, one instance per size): `dirn100m060r150s06i03` (n=100), `dirn200m120r150s06i00` (n=198), `dirn400m240r150s06i00` (n=400).
Runs are seeded `srand(run+1)` (the baseline's CLI seed is dead code), so run *r* starts from the same pre-processed solution for every κ → paired comparisons.

| cell | runs | source log dir |
|---|---|---|
| n=100, κ∈{2,4,6,8} | 10 | `out/p1` |
| n=198, κ∈{2,4,6,8} | 10 | `out/p1b` (the 5-run n=198 cells in `out/p1` are the first five of these seeds and are not used in the tables below) |
| n=400, κ∈{2,4,6,8} | **3** | `out/p1` |
| no-op decomposition, n=100 and 198, κ∈{2,4,8} | 3 | `out/noop` (this work) |

**Metrics.** (M1) share of offspring with |f_off − f_parent| < 1e-7 (tie), and better/worse shares, over iterations 1000–3000 of the 4,000. (M3) *Equal-evaluation best-so-far*:
for each run, B* = the smallest total candidate-evaluation count over the four κ of that run; compare best-so-far costs at B* and at 25 % / 50 % of B*; differences are paired by run
and tested with Wilcoxon signed-rank (exploratory, two-sided, no multiplicity correction; reported only for ≥5 runs). (M4) Strict incumbent gain (percent of start cost) per 1000 evaluations in four budget quarters.
(M5, new) among offspring: identical permutation (true no-op) vs different permutation with equal cost (plateau move); and the share of evaluations spent on identical-permutation mutations.
Decision rules for the broader probe were fixed beforehand in `docs/DECISION_RULES_PHASE1.md`.

Going from κ=2 to κ=8 raises the worse-offspring share 30× (n=100), 22× (n=198), 25× (n=400), while the better-offspring share changes by +0.3, +0.3 and −0.5 percentage points. Evaluations per

Run-to-run noise (`docs/data/paired_spread.txt`): the between-run SD of κ=4's best at B* is 1.62 % (n=100), 0.77 % (n=198), 0.47 % (n=400) of the mean; the SD of the paired
κ-vs-κ=4 difference is 1.8–2.2 % (n=100), 0.8–1.0 % (n=198), 0.2–0.8 % (n=400). Standard errors of the mean paired difference at full budget are 0.57–0.70 % (n=100),
0.26–0.32 % (n=198), 0.12–0.44 % (n=400). Hence:

* n=100: κ=6 and 8 are about 0.6 % better at full budget, ≈1 SE; not distinguishable from zero (p=0.38, 0.28).
* n=198: all κ end within 0.08 % of κ=4. The only significant effects are *early* and *against* larger κ (κ=6 at 25 % and 50 % of B*; p=0.027, 0.014, uncorrected; 9 tests at n=198 → ≈0.45 false positives expected at α=0.05 if all effects were null, and 18 tests over n=100 and n=198 together).
* n=400: κ=2 is 0.36 % better at full budget in 3 of 3 runs (mean ≈3 SE; with 3 runs a Wilcoxon test cannot give p<0.25, and a t-test would give p≈0.09 uncorrected), but the same κ=2 is 0.43 % *worse* at 25 % of the budget in 3 of 3 runs. This is the only observation that might be a real κ effect, and it is not established.

### 3.3 Gain per evaluation (Fig. 4, `fig4_gain_per_1000_evals.png`)

Strict improvements of the incumbent per 1000 evaluations: κ=2 has the highest value in the first quarter at every size (n=100: 0.0410 vs 0.0208 for κ=4; n=198: 0.0126 vs 0.0069; n=400: 0.0052 vs 0.0032, % of start cost per 1000 evals)
and is also the highest in every later quarter at all three sizes (Fig. 4); κ=8 is lowest or near lowest nearly everywhere. The only rank changes are among κ=4/6/8 and are small (e.g. n=198 Q2: κ=2 0.0016, κ=6 0.0009, κ=4 0.0007, κ=8 0.0005).
This efficiency advantage of small κ per evaluation does **not** translate into better best-so-far at the full budget (§3.2), because improvements per evaluation shrink quickly after the first quarter whichever κ is used.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n=100 | 2 | 99.3 % | 18.2 % | 81.1 % | 18.2 % (whole run incl. shuffles: 17.1 %) |
| n=100 | 4 | 95.1 % | 3.9 % | 91.2 % | 3.9 % (4.1 %) |

| n=198 | 2 | 98.7 % | 42.5 % | 56.1 % | 42.5 % (40.0 %) |

(The identical-permutation mutations share the same per-mutation cost as any other mutation at the same κ, so their evaluation share equals their mutation share; the whole-run figure in brackets includes the shuffle evaluations in the denominator.)

**Correction to the earlier statement** in `docs/DECISION.md` ("most of the evaluation budget is spent on no-op moves"): that was inferred from the 92–98 % tie rate and is **too strong**. At the published setting κ=4, between 3.9 % (n=100) and 16.4 % (n=198) of the evaluation budget is spent on provably identical-permutation mutations; the remaining ≈80–91 % of mutations are plateau moves. Because the baseline code accepts ties, those moves change the incumbent permutation (with equal cost). Whether this neutral drift is useful or wasted is **not measured** here (it needs an ablation with strict acceptance, which the paper's text describes and the code does not implement).

## 4. Interpretation

1. Within the tested family (uniform destroy, greedy reinsertion, κ ∈ {2..8}), the 4,000-iteration run converges to practically the same quality regardless of κ **when compared at equal candidate evaluations**. Differences are of the size of the run-to-run noise (SE 0.3–0.7 %), except the early disadvantage of κ≥6 at n=198 and a small, unconfirmed late advantage of κ=2 at n=400.
2. Mechanism consistent with the logs: the greedy repair almost always rebuilds a solution of the same cost. Most mutations return an equal-cost permutation (frequently a different one), so a bigger hole only increases the chance of a *worse* rebuilt solution (×22–30 from κ=2 to 8) without increasing improvements, while evaluation cost grows ∝ κ. This is the pattern the design document's §25 flags as "insensitive landscape" — but only for κ, and we have not tested the selection rule.
3. Upper bound on what κ-adaptation can buy here: at n=198 no fixed κ beats κ=4 at the full budget (best case κ=2, −0.00 %); a schedule could in principle beat all fixed κ only through phase-dependent switching, and the per-quarter ranking by gain/evaluation is practically constant (κ=2 first), which gives a phase-switching controller little to learn. This is an argument from the logs, not a proof.

## 5. Threats to validity

* **One instance per size, one aperture (φ=π/3).** The three instances share the `s06` family. Results may differ for φ=2π/3 or π/2, other instances, and n≤50 where ES-TRMP already ties.
* **Few runs at n=400 (3).** Pairing helps, but no test has power; all n=400 statements are descriptive. A 10-run n=400 κ=2-vs-κ=4 check is the obvious follow-up (the only borderline observation).
* **Exploratory statistics.** p-values are uncorrected two-sided Wilcoxon tests (smallest attainable p with 10 pairs ≈ 0.002); the two p<0.05 results in §3.2 are early-budget, against larger κ, and the smaller of the two (p=0.014) would not survive a Bonferroni correction over the 18 Wilcoxon tests shown (threshold 0.0028).
* **No-op decomposition uses 3 runs per cell** (the first three seeds of the 10-run cells; tie rates differ from the 10-run values by at most 1.0 percentage point) and covers only n=100 and n=198, κ∈{2,4,8}.
* **Scope: κ only.** The probe does not vary *which* groups are destroyed (cost-greedy, history-aware, block), the shuffle policy (η, δ ladder), the acceptance rule (strict vs tie-accepting), nor the repair operator. A controller acting on those is untested; the null here must not be read as covering them.
* **Evaluation unit.** Every `findcost_d` call counts as one evaluation, but a call scans a prefix of between n−κ+1 and n groups, so call cost varies by at most about κ/n between κ values (≈8 % at n=100, κ=8). The scanned-prefix counter `eval_units` exists in the refactored driver (`src/es_drl.c`), not in the diagnostic binaries, so the effect was not measured here.
* **Measure design.** Gain per 1000 evaluations counts strict improvements only; plateau moves, which could matter for later improvements, are not credited.
* **Instrumentation fidelity.** The refactored driver matches the original on n=18, 25, 198 (exact rows); the diagnostic binaries are generated from the original source by string patches and reproduced the earlier per-run bests exactly in the cells checked, but a full equivalence check at n=400 was still pending at the time of writing.

## 6. Implications

* **For an adaptive (RL) controller on destruction size.** Do not expect more than ≈0.2 % mean gain from κ alone at n=198; n=100 shows ≈0.6 % but it is within 1 SE. If a controller is built, report it as an *evaluation-budget-matched* test with ≥20 runs, and expect a negative result to be informative, as the design document says. The logged quantities (tie share, worse share rising ∝ κ, gain per evaluation) already show a cleaner story than any Q-table would: small κ is more efficient per evaluation early, and nothing in the data says large κ ever pays.
* **Where there may still be signal** (untested, so only hypotheses): (a) *which* groups are destroyed — 80–91 % of mutations at κ=4 produce an equal-cost permutation, which suggests (not shown) that many groups can be reordered without changing cost; destroying groups whose position actually affects cost (cost-greedy / history-aware) could raise the improvement rate; (b) the shuffle trigger/size; (c) acceptance of plateau moves; (d) avoiding the identical-permutation mutations (3.9–16.4 % of the budget at κ=4, ≈40 % at κ=2, n=198) by detecting and re-drawing them — a cheap, measurable saving that needs no learning.
* **For the G1 quantum-inspired project.** The comparison baseline should be the *equal-evaluation* ES-TRMP, since the κ-sweep shows that evaluation counts, not iteration counts, decide fairness; and the plateau structure (many equal-cost permutations) suggests that G1 should look at the neutral-move structure and the selection of *which* groups interact, rather than at the step size.
* **For the Differential-RL project.** Option C stands as the fallback paper contribution if the cheap follow-ups (n=400 κ=2 vs 4; a fixed-policy probe for selection) are also flat; if either shows a gap, the Q-learner is justified on that action, not on κ.
