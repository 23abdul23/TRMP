\section*{Adopted}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Contiguous (\texttt{block}) destroy with $\kappa=4$}: the only change with a robust, large, size-dependent gain; no extra evaluations; the controls show contiguity is the cause.
\item \textbf{Equal-evaluation accounting and paired seeds}: prevents an action that merely spends more computation from looking better and removes start-point variance.
\item \textbf{Pre-registered decision rules and control arms}: prevent results from being fitted after the fact; they exposed that learning $\kappa$ has no headroom.
\item \textbf{Instrumented driver with exact-reproduction and equivalence tests}: every number is traceable to code that matches the original.
\item \textbf{The paper's protocol for the headline claim and a same-session baseline for the paired confirmation.}
\end{itemize}

\section*{Dropped}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Learning $\kappa$ with differential or discounted Q-learning}: $\kappa$ has no headroom; the learner equals constant-$\kappa$ controls and is worse than fixed $\kappa=2$ at $n=400$.
\item \textbf{\texttt{cost-greedy} destroy}: no gain at any size; \texttt{block} removes cheaper groups than uniform yet wins, so cost is not the mechanism.
\item \textbf{\texttt{history-aware} destroy}: no effect at $n\le198$; $-0.29\%$ at $n=400$ on five runs ($p=0.0625$), worse early; unproven.
\item \textbf{Mixing uniform and \texttt{block}}: pure \texttt{block} beats every mix.
\item \textbf{A learner over operators and a learned shuffle}: no state-dependent preference resolvable at $n\le198$; not built.
\item \textbf{Raw-evaluation time unit for $\tau$}: the estimate $\bar R$ diverged; replaced by the evaluations of one $\kappa=4$ mutation.
\item \textbf{The claim that differential learning is better than discounted learning}: not supported against the controls.
\item \textbf{A re-run of GA-TRMP+LSB}: skipped on instruction.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Corrected statements}

\begin{itemize}
\item \enquote{Most of the evaluation budget is wasted on no-operations}: true no-operations are only about $4\%$ ($n=100$) to $16\%$ ($n=198$) of the budget at $\kappa=4$; most ties are plateau moves.
\item \enquote{$\kappa=2$ beats $\kappa=4$ at $n=400$} ($-0.36\%$ and $-0.74\%$ on three runs): noise; ten paired runs give $+0.05\%$ ($p=0.77$).
\item The shuffle size is $\lfloor n/7\rfloor$ in both the paper and the code (the design brief says $\lceil n/7\rceil$).
\end{itemize}
