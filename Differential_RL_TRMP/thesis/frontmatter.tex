\begin{abstract}
\begin{spacing}{1.3}

\addchaptertocentry{\abstractname} % Add the abstract to the table of contents

The Total Rotation Minimization Problem (TRMP) asks for the activation order of sensor groups in a directional sensor network that minimises the total angular rotation of the sensors. It is NP-hard, and the rotation needed for a group depends on several earlier groups, so costs cannot be tabulated. The state-of-the-art metaheuristic, ES-TRMP, is a $(1+1)$ evolution strategy whose mutation destroys $\kappa=4$ uniformly random groups and repairs the schedule greedily. This thesis asks whether this blind destroy step can be improved by a learned controller.

\pronounwe{} rebuilt ES-TRMP and reproduced its published results exactly on 33 instances, built an instrumented driver that is bit-identical to the original, and evaluated every variant under an equal-evaluation budget with paired seeds and pre-registered decision rules. A tabular differential (average-reward, semi-Markov) Q-learning controller, and a discounted variant, that choose the destruction size were tested against fixed-$\kappa$ and random-$\kappa$ controls. They learned a constant policy and matched the controls: the destruction size has almost no effect on solution quality at equal compute, so there was nothing to learn.

The controlled experiments instead exposed a different lever, namely \emph{which} groups are removed. Removing a contiguous block of four adjacent groups, a fixed non-learned change, improves ES-TRMP on all 26 instances with at least 198 groups (mean $-2.17\%$, range $-1.18\%$ to $-3.07\%$; $-2.32\%$ on the 15 instances with 400 groups; Wilcoxon $p<0.0001$) under the original protocol of 4{,}000 iterations and 20 runs. A same-session paired re-run confirms the result in 157 of 160 paired runs. The change costs no additional evaluations, leaves small instances unchanged and does not reduce run-to-run variance. The thesis records what was tried, what was dropped and why, and attributes the improvement to the contiguity of the removed set and not to learning.

\end{spacing}
\end{abstract}
